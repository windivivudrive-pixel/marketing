# Scoring and safety

## Duplicate fields

1. viewer pain or question;
2. case or story;
3. branding lesson;
4. practical payoff;
5. target emotion.

Mark duplicate when at least three substantially match. Mark `ADJACENT` when two match and state the truly new angle.

## Evidence

- Use current platform/search signals for trend claims.
- A `VIRAL` label requires a direct signal and observation date. Popularity inferred from vibes is not evidence.
- Use primary or authoritative sources for medical, food-safety, legal, finance, public policy, and product specifications.
- Separate public statements, reporting, and unverified claims.
- Never use a public person's image or silence as proof of an allegation.

## General guardrails

Do not diagnose, promise financial results, invent personal outcomes, shame identity/body/relationships, exploit minors or private people, or turn coercion and abuse into entertainment. Flag sensitive topics as `RESEARCH_REQUIRED` before scripting.

Do not copy another creator's narration, topic sequence, thumbnail, distinctive visual treatment, or recurring branded phrase. A reference channel may inform the abstract story-to-lesson structure only.

## Everyday-story fit

Prefer an idea when a viewer can recognize the opening without marketing knowledge, the opening creates a real unanswered question, and the explanation naturally opens into several literal scenes: people, actions, places, objects, interfaces, or consequences. A recurring motif may connect those scenes, but it must not replace them.

Reject an idea when more than half of its likely cues would show the same object plus different labels, when motion only means moving or scaling that object, or when voice subjects such as customer, staff, friend, delivery, comparison, and test have no literal visual opportunity.

The reference may teach the abstract grammar `familiar object → curiosity → mechanism → examples → lesson → application`. Do not reuse its object, wording, example order, palette, typography, language, footage, or identity.

## Phenomenon-led fit

When the profile uses `business-phenomenon-decode-v1`, prefer a candidate only when a non-marketer can understand the first question, make a prediction, and care about the consequence. A theory label is not a curiosity gap.

Require proof proportional to the claim. A documented experiment may support a measured effect; a plausible shop observation supports only an observation. Reject precise percentages, sales lifts, physical specifications, brand practices, or causal claims when the cited source does not directly establish them.

Across the retention resets, require new information rather than new decoration. The plan must visibly move from puzzle to action through story, proof, and mechanism; it must not become a sequence of generic text boards.
